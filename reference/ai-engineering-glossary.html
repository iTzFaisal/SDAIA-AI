System language

TermUse it to mean
Data contractThe explicit agreement about inputs, schemas, provenance, quality, ownership, and allowed use.
Data leakageInformation crossing into training or model selection from a source that would not be available at prediction time.
GeneralizationPerformance on relevant unseen examples, not merely fit on the training data.
Error analysisStructured examination of failures to discover patterns, risks, and the next improvement.
Retrieval-augmented generationA generation pattern that fetches relevant external context at query time and supplies it to a generative model.
LLMOpsThe practices for releasing and operating systems that use large language models, including prompt, context, quality, cost, and safety management.
Model servingMaking a model available for prediction through a controlled runtime boundary.
DriftA change in data, relationships, or behavior that can make a previously acceptable system less reliable.
SLOA target for a service-level indicator, such as latency or availability, that guides operational decisions.
Threat modelA structured account of assets, actors, attack paths, impacts, and controls for a system.
GovernanceThe roles, policies, evidence, review points, and decisions that make accountable AI use possible.
Graceful degradationA planned reduction in capability that preserves safety or core service when resources or dependencies fail.
Integrated capstoneThe small document-intelligence/RAG service used to connect the seven domains during study.

How to use the glossary

When answering a scenario, name the domain decision first, then name the evidence that would change it. For example: "This is a validation problem; I need a time-aware split before comparing metrics." Precise nouns make hidden assumptions visible.

Definitions are course vocabulary, not official exam wording. Return to the course home.
