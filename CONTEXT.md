# AI Engineer Expert Exam Preparation

This context defines the language for the teaching workspace. It keeps the exam target, learning routes, and readiness claims precise across lessons and reference pages.

## Credential

**AI Engineer Expert badge**:
The target credential represented by the supplied `AI-Badge.png`. In this workspace it is the reason for learning, not a claim about any learner's current level.

**Exam blueprint**:
The seven domains and weights shown in `AI-Badge.png`, including its claimed 140-question and 3.5-hour format.
_Avoid_: syllabus, official item allocation (unless separately published)

**Exam domain**:
One of the seven capability areas used to organize study and practice.
_Avoid_: subject, chapter (when referring to the assessment structure)

## Learning

**Diagnostic**:
A short evidence-gathering activity that routes a learner to Foundation, Core, or Accelerated study. It is not a pass prediction.

**Foundation route**:
The route for repairing prerequisite knowledge before tackling the standard Expert sequence.

**Core route**:
The standard route through explanation, practice, and integration for a learner with the assumed prerequisites.

**Accelerated route**:
The route that compresses exposition and increases scenario, integration, and mock-exam practice for a learner who can already demonstrate the foundations.

**Domain lab**:
A bounded practical exercise that makes one exam domain observable through an artifact, experiment, or design decision.

**Integrated capstone**:
The small document-intelligence/RAG service used to connect all seven domains in one production-style scenario.

**Preparation standard**:
The internal readiness target of 80% in every domain, 85% on two complete timed mocks, and defensible system trade-offs. It is not an official pass mark.

## Evidence

**Error log**:
A record of missed or uncertain questions, the underlying misconception, and the next corrective exercise.
