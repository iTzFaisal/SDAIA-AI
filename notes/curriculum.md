# Curriculum & study schedule

> Independent, unofficial preparation. Not affiliated with or endorsed by SDAIA. Questions are original practice material, not actual examination questions. Topic breakdowns and practice timings are our design choices, not official exam specifications.

Reviewed: 2026-10-12.

## Release status

Available: the foundation primer, 42 lessons across Domains 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 126 lesson questions, and a separate 24-question paper for each available domain. Mocks 1–3 are available with 150 fresh questions each across all seven domains, in separate reserved sets. Detailed topic lists are inferred curriculum choices, not a verified official syllabus.

## Learning path

### Domain 1: Data, ML Foundations & Model Evaluation (17.3%)

Status: Lessons and paper available.

Turn a business question into an honest, useful model evaluation.

**Proposed topics**

- Features, labels, learning types, and basic statistics
- Data quality, missing values, encoding, scaling, and imbalance
- Training/validation/test splits, leakage, and cross-validation
- Baseline models, common algorithms, fitting, and generalization
- Confusion matrices, precision, recall, F1, and thresholds
- Regression metrics, ranking, calibration, slices, and model selection

**Learning objectives**

- Identify the learning task and information available at prediction time.
- Choose a defensible preparation and splitting strategy.
- Recognize leakage, overfitting, and underfitting.
- Calculate basic metrics and justify them using error costs.
- Select a model using validation evidence without contaminating the final test.

### Domain 2: Core AI, Deep Learning & Generative AI (16.7%)

Status: Lessons and paper available.

Understand how modern AI represents information and produces outputs.

**Proposed topics**

- AI approaches and search at a conceptual level
- Neural networks, activations, loss, and backpropagation
- CNNs, sequence models, attention, and transformers
- Tokens, embeddings, and language-model behavior
- Prompting, RAG, fine-tuning, and model selection
- Hallucination, grounding, evaluation, and agent limitations

**Learning objectives**

- Explain training versus inference and the role of learned representations.
- Choose an appropriate model family for a stated problem.
- Compare prompting, retrieval, and fine-tuning.
- Design a meaningful evaluation for a generative use case.

### Domain 3: AI Software Engineering & Integration (14.0%)

Status: Lessons and paper available.

Make AI a reliable part of a larger application.

**Proposed topics**

- APIs, HTTP concepts, schemas, and data contracts
- Synchronous and asynchronous workflows
- Testing layers, mocks, reproducibility, and failure handling
- Model adapters, dependency management, and version compatibility
- Retrieval integrations, tool permissions, and structured outputs
- Documentation, code review concepts, and maintainability

**Learning objectives**

- Explain how applications exchange inputs and model outputs.
- Recognize integration failure modes and appropriate tests.
- Choose safeguards for unreliable or malformed AI output.
- Describe a maintainable integration without writing code.

### Domain 4: MLOps, LLMOps, Deployment & Reliability (18.0%)

Status: Lessons and paper available.

Keep models useful and dependable after launch.

**Proposed topics**

- Experiment tracking and data/model/prompt versioning
- Registries, pipelines, CI/CD concepts, and release gates
- Batch and online serving; shadow, canary, and blue-green releases
- Monitoring, drift, delayed labels, and retraining decisions
- SLOs, alerts, fallbacks, rollback, and incident response
- LLM evaluations, retrieval freshness, cost, and tracing

**Learning objectives**

- Describe a reproducible model release process.
- Select deployment and rollback strategies for a risk profile.
- Distinguish data drift, concept drift, and operational failures.
- Choose actionable monitoring and reliability measures.

### Domain 5: Architecture, Infrastructure, Performance & Scalability (12.6%)

Status: Lessons and paper available.

Balance quality, speed, scale, availability, and cost.

**Proposed topics**

- End-to-end AI architecture and storage choices
- CPU/GPU concepts, memory, networking, and workload fit
- Latency percentiles, throughput, batching, and caching
- Scaling, queues, load balancing, and capacity planning
- Quantization, distillation, and quality/performance tradeoffs
- Cloud/on-premises/hybrid choices and resilience

**Learning objectives**

- Identify bottlenecks from system symptoms.
- Choose serving patterns and infrastructure for workload constraints.
- Compare performance techniques without assuming free quality gains.
- Explain resilience and capacity decisions.

### Domain 6: Responsible AI, Security & Governance (14.7%)

Status: Lessons and paper available.

Build justified trust and control risks throughout the lifecycle.

**Proposed topics**

- Fairness, transparency, explainability, and human oversight
- Privacy, minimization, purpose limitation, and Saudi PDPL context
- SDAIA ethics guidance and governance roles
- Threat modeling, access control, secrets, and supply-chain risk
- Prompt injection, poisoning, exfiltration, and tool abuse
- Risk assessments, documentation, auditability, and incident escalation

**Learning objectives**

- Identify ethical, privacy, and security risks in a scenario.
- Select layered safeguards and appropriate human oversight.
- Explain governance responsibilities and useful documentation.
- Apply current Saudi guidance carefully, separating legal requirements from advice.

### Domain 7: Business, Professional Practice & Collaboration (6.7%)

Status: Lessons and paper available.

Connect technical choices to real needs and responsible teamwork.

**Proposed topics**

- Problem discovery, feasibility, and non-AI alternatives
- Success criteria, business KPIs, ROI, and total cost
- Stakeholder communication and uncertainty
- Team roles, handoffs, documentation, and collaboration
- Project prioritization and change management
- Professional integrity, escalation, and continuous learning

**Learning objectives**

- Translate a vague request into measurable success criteria.
- Evaluate whether AI is justified rather than fashionable.
- Communicate limitations and risks without overstating certainty.
- Recommend practical collaboration and escalation steps.

## Eight-week schedule

Plan for 10–14 hours weekly. This is intensive preparation, not a guarantee of Expert readiness from zero.

### 12–18 Oct: Start from zero

Learn the foundation primer. Study features, labels, averages, and the model lifecycle. Begin lessons 1–2.

Target: 10–14 hours.

### 19–25 Oct: Data, ML & evaluation

Finish Domain 1. Complete lesson quizzes, then the unseen domain paper. Explain every missed answer aloud.

Target: 10–14 hours.

### 26 Oct–1 Nov: Core AI, deep learning & GenAI

Study Domain 2: AI approaches, neural networks, transformers, embeddings, prompting, RAG, fine-tuning, and generative evaluation. Complete the separate Domain 2 paper and repair missed concepts.

Target: 10–14 hours.

### 2–8 Nov: Integration & architecture

Study Domain 3: contracts, asynchronous work, failure handling, testing, safe tools, and maintainability. Complete its separate paper. Preview Domain 5's architecture and compute lessons; finish Domain 4 before its later capacity and recovery cases.

Target: 10–14 hours.

### 9–15 Nov: Operations, performance & reliability

Study Domain 4's releases, monitoring, and incidents, then Domain 5's performance measurements, batching/caching, capacity, placement, and recovery. Split the weekly hours between both domains, complete their separate papers, and use revision time to repair missed decisions rather than rushing.

Target: 10–14 hours.

### 16–22 Nov: Responsible AI & business

Study Domain 6: fairness, oversight, privacy and Saudi PDPL context, threat boundaries, prompt injection, and governance. Study Domain 7: problem discovery, business measures, cost/benefit assumptions, uncertainty, collaboration, change, and professional integrity. Split the hours across both domains, take their separate papers, and repair weak decisions during revision rather than rushing.

Target: 10–14 hours.

### 23–29 Nov: Mixed revision & mock 1

Take the available Mock 1 after studying all seven domains. Use a first attempt without the key, then repair weak concepts using its every-option explanations. Retaking this mock is familiar practice, not another fresh assessment.

Target: 10–14 hours.

### 30 Nov–4 Dec: Mocks 2–3 & final review

Take Mock 2 after repairing Mock 1's weak concepts. Use Mock 3's separate reserved scenarios only after further repair, without its key, if time permits within the weekly target. Do not crowd out revision or rest to finish every mock; retakes use familiar questions. Finish with targeted revision, then light review. Confirm center, ID, arrival time, and official instructions.

Target: 10–14 hours.

## Assessment design

- Separate lesson checks, domain papers, and reserved mock pools. Explanations cover every option.
- Mocks 1–3 are available with 150 fresh questions each across all seven domains, in separate reserved sets.
- Each available mock uses the proposed 150-question allocation: 26 / 25 / 21 / 27 / 19 / 22 / 10 across Domains 1–7. This approximates the image weights; 150 is not an official verified exam length.
- Suggested practice time per mock: 180 minutes, not a verified official duration. Timed mode hides feedback until submission; learning mode is supported study.
- Each question carries one point. Weighting comes from domain question counts, not an additional domain multiplier. Unanswered questions count as incorrect.
- Do not map practice scores or our difficulty labels to official badge levels.
- Retakes use familiar questions and do not measure the same thing as a first attempt at fresh questions.
