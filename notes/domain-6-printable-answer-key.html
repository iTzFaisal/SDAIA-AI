1. Which statement best connects SDAIA's published ethics principles to AI development?

  1. They matter only after a public complaint
  2. Fairness alone replaces privacy and safety
  3. Listing them certifies official exam readiness
  4. They guide concrete controls from planning through deployment and monitoring

Correct: D.

  • A: The framework connects ethical controls to the entire system lifecycle.
  • B: The principles address multiple distinct interacting concerns.
  • C: They are an ethics framework, not badge thresholds or a readiness guarantee.
  • D: Lifecycle decisions, owners, and evidence operationalize the published principles.

2. A model is trained to predict past loan approval, and historical approvals excluded qualified applicants from one region. What should be investigated first?

  1. Whether deeper layers can copy approval history more accurately
  2. Whether the label represents a defensible desired outcome and whether coverage is adequate
  3. Whether more duplicate approval records will guarantee fairness
  4. Whether deleting the region name alone eliminates all related influence

Correct: B.

  • A: Better reproduction of a biased decision label may preserve the original harm.
  • B: Label meaning and historical exclusion can create unfairness before algorithm choice.
  • C: More copies do not repair a biased target or missing populations.
  • D: Other inputs can remain proxies and the label can still encode exclusion.

3. In a fictional screen, Group A has 12 false positives among 60 actual negatives; Group B has 12 among 30. What are their false-positive rates?

  1. A 20%; B 40%
  2. Both 12% because counts match
  3. A 40%; B 20%
  4. A 80%; B 60%

Correct: A.

  • A: False-positive rates use actual negatives: 12/60 = 20% and 12/30 = 40%.
  • B: Equal counts can correspond to different rates when denominators differ.
  • C: This reverses the correct group denominators.
  • D: Those are the complementary true-negative fractions, not false-positive rates.

4. Two groups have equal selection rates but different recall, prevalence, and error consequences. What is the most defensible fairness conclusion?

  1. Equal selection proves every aspect of fairness
  2. One threshold must make every fairness metric equal
  3. Inspect valid labels, counts, uncertainty, consequences, and contextual tradeoffs
  4. Tune repeatedly on the reserved test until every rate matches

Correct: C.

  • A: One parity measure does not settle error behavior, consequences, or justified treatment.
  • B: Fairness goals can conflict under imperfect prediction and differing prevalence.
  • C: A defensible assessment relates multiple measures and evidence to affected people.
  • D: Test-driven tuning contaminates evaluation and may not resolve the underlying problem.

5. What is the best distinction between a model card and a system-level record?

  1. Both guarantee legal compliance regardless of application
  2. A model card must reveal all production secrets
  3. The system record also covers the model's retrieval, tools, users, oversight, and downstream use
  4. Neither needs to match the deployed version

Correct: C.

  • A: Documentation supplies evidence and limits, not a universal compliance guarantee.
  • B: Useful transparency does not require unauthorized disclosure of secrets or private data.
  • C: A complete application introduces responsibilities and risks beyond the model artifact.
  • D: Stale documentation can misrepresent the process actually producing decisions.

6. A feature-contribution report says commuting distance influenced a hiring prediction. What must not be inferred from that alone?

  1. Moving closer will causally guarantee hiring
  2. The explanation concerns a model result
  3. The actual input and model version should be checked
  4. The audience needs an understandable account

Correct: A.

  • A: A post-hoc contribution is not proof of a causal, feasible, or guaranteed intervention.
  • B: That is its intended subject, though fidelity still needs evaluation.
  • C: Versioned evidence helps validate the explanation and decision.
  • D: Audience-appropriate communication is part of meaningful transparency.

7. A team must review 160 decisions/hour. Each takes 2 minutes and four reviewers work at the ideal rate. What is the capacity gap?

  1. No gap; four reviewers complete 240/hour
  2. Only 4 decisions/hour can be reviewed
  3. 80 decisions/hour of spare capacity
  4. Capacity 120/hour; backlog grows 40/hour before other overhead

Correct: D.

  • A: Each reviewer completes 30/hour, not 60 under a two-minute review time.
  • B: Reviewer count is not the completion rate.
  • C: The required rate exceeds the ideal 120/hour capacity rather than falling below it.
  • D: 4 × (60/2) = 120, leaving 160 − 120 = 40 per hour under the stated assumptions.

8. An appeal button asks the same unchecked model to repeat its original recommendation. What is the strongest improvement?

  1. Generate a longer rationale without changing the process
  2. Route to qualified review with source evidence and authority to correct or reverse the decision
  3. Hide disagreement so users cannot be confused
  4. Publish other applicants' records to explain the ranking

Correct: B.

  • A: More persuasive wording does not create independent reconsideration.
  • B: A meaningful challenge route must be able to investigate and change the outcome.
  • C: Concealing uncertainty and challenge outcomes undermines transparency and redress.
  • D: Redress must still protect other people's privacy and authorization boundaries.

9. A document has no names but a unique location, role, and event date identify its subject. How should it be treated?

  1. Anonymous solely because names were removed
  2. As potentially personal data because indirect identification remains possible
  3. As automatically exempt because it is converted to an embedding
  4. As a secret-free artifact requiring no controls

Correct: B.

  • A: Indirect identifiers can still make an individual identifiable.
  • B: PDPL's personal-data definition is broader than explicit names.
  • C: A derived representation can preserve or reveal identifying information.
  • D: Identifiability and confidentiality need actual assessment, not format assumptions.

10. A retailer determines why and how customer messages are classified, using a contracted supplier solely on its behalf. Which role description fits those stated facts?

  1. The retailer becomes only a processor by outsourcing
  2. No party has privacy responsibility if an API is used
  3. The supplier's marketing label overrides its actual conduct
  4. The retailer is the controller; the supplier acts as its processor for this use

Correct: D.

  • A: Outsourcing does not change who determines processing purposes and methods.
  • B: An API changes the workflow, not the existence of responsibilities.
  • C: Roles depend on actual processing purposes and arrangements.
  • D: The stated purpose-setting and on-behalf-of processing align with those roles, while controller duties remain.

11. A verified destruction request applies, but an older recovery copy still contains the personal data. What is the best plan?

  1. Delete only the search result and declare all copies destroyed
  2. Ignore backups because restoration is an operational matter
  3. Coordinate applicable destruction and exceptions across copies and prevent unsafe reintroduction on restore
  4. Keep every copy forever because processing records have a retention rule

Correct: C.

  • A: A derived index is not the complete source, supplier, cache, and backup copy map.
  • B: Destruction requirements address copies including backups subject to applicable requirements.
  • C: Privacy and recovery owners must handle the complete data lifecycle rather than just the visible entry.
  • D: Processing-activity metadata retention does not justify indefinite retention of every payload.

12. A hosted-model provider offers encryption and a chosen region, but support access, logging locations, and onward providers are unknown. Which conclusion is justified?

  1. The full processing/transfer map and applicable current protection route still need qualified review
  2. All cross-border personal-data transfers are always prohibited
  3. Encryption alone permits any destination
  4. A region label guarantees every obligation is satisfied

Correct: A.

  • A: Encryption and one location setting do not establish all destinations, access, or legal conditions.
  • B: Saudi law provides conditional transfer rules rather than that universal summary.
  • C: Encryption is a safeguard, not a complete legal authorization.
  • D: Local placement does not establish purposes, basis, rights handling, or supplier practices.

13. Which entry is most useful in an AI assistant's threat model?

  1. Public upload → internal retrieval boundary → possible private-data disclosure → enforced controls and owner
  2. A list of GPU brands without data paths
  3. The statement that high accuracy prevents all attacks
  4. Only the final user-visible answer

Correct: A.

  • A: This connects an entry path and trust boundary to a concrete consequence and mitigation responsibility.
  • B: Hardware names alone do not identify adversaries, authority, or disclosure paths.
  • C: Prediction quality is not an enforced security boundary.
  • D: Ingestion, model context, tools, network destinations, and logs also matter.

14. An attacker can submit ratings that are automatically promoted into training labels. Which safeguard best addresses this poisoning path?

  1. Increase automatic ingestion speed
  2. Treat every submitted rating as verified truth
  3. Validate feedback origin and meaning, control promotion, and evaluate against independent evidence
  4. Keep only a public-average accuracy chart

Correct: C.

  • A: Faster ingestion can promote hostile feedback more quickly.
  • B: User-controlled feedback is not automatically defensible outcome evidence.
  • C: These controls protect the learning inputs and detect harmful candidate behavior.
  • D: An aggregate can hide targeted corruption and does not protect data promotion.

15. An application authenticates a user, then lets the model request any tenant's file ID. What is missing?

  1. A stronger statement that the model should be polite
  2. Authorization binding each requested resource to the current user's allowed scope
  3. A requirement to hide the file ID after retrieval
  4. A faster accelerator to reduce decision latency

Correct: B.

  • A: Prompt wording does not enforce resource-specific permissions.
  • B: Identity alone does not permit access to every tenant's records.
  • C: Hiding output does not undo unauthorized input access or later disclosure paths.
  • D: Compute speed does not establish data-access permission.

16. A popular third-party model has high average quality but unknown provenance and loading code. What is the sound selection process?

  1. Load it on the production secret-bearing host immediately
  2. Assume popularity resolves licensing and security
  3. Evaluate only whether its file is smaller
  4. Verify trusted origin, terms/licenses, integrity, isolated loading risks, and task/security evidence

Correct: D.

  • A: Unknown artifacts and associated loading code can create a compromise path.
  • B: Popularity is not evidence of authorized use or harmless behavior.
  • C: Size does not establish origin, safety, rights, or task suitability.
  • D: Supply-chain diligence must cover more than an aggregate benchmark and repository reputation.

17. A legitimate user asks for a summary; a retrieved attachment says it has administrative authority to change recipients. Which treatment is correct?

  1. Accept its role claim because the file was retrieved
  2. Treat the role claim as real approval for any tool
  3. Assume RAG eliminated injection before the model saw it
  4. Treat the claim as untrusted content and enforce real permissions outside the model

Correct: D.

  • A: Retrieval does not grant application authority to content.
  • B: External text cannot bind authorized approval of a consequential action.
  • C: RAG can itself supply hostile content and does not guarantee prevention.
  • D: A source can provide task evidence without controlling the application's authority.

18. A security test reports 0 successful violations in 100 known attacks but rejects 70 of 100 legitimate tasks. What is the best interpretation?

  1. Universal immunity with perfect usefulness
  2. Limited security evidence and a substantial utility problem requiring further evaluation
  3. Exactly 70% attack success
  4. Permission controls can now be removed

Correct: B.

  • A: The limited set cannot prove immunity, and most legitimate tasks failed.
  • B: Both actual violations and beneficial-task completion matter; known tests do not cover all threats.
  • C: Legitimate-task rejection is a different measure from attack success.
  • D: Passing a finite test set cannot justify removing the enforced boundaries.

19. An assistant displays a generated remote image whose address contains private context. Why can a text-only output filter be insufficient?

  1. Rendering can initiate an unauthorized outbound request that leaks information
  2. Images automatically anonymize every address
  3. A read-only viewer cannot disclose any data
  4. Valid Markdown establishes destination authorization

Correct: A.

  • A: Disclosure paths include generated links/media and network requests, not only visible prose.
  • B: A URL can carry identifying or confidential information regardless of displayed media.
  • C: Reading and rendering can still expose information to outside destinations.
  • D: Format validity does not establish that a network destination is permitted.

20. A user approves sending a specific invoice to Supplier A. Before execution, the model changes the recipient to Supplier B. What is required?

  1. Reuse approval because the tool name is unchanged
  2. Accept a retrieved note claiming the user agreed
  3. Reject the changed operation until permission checks and fresh exact-action approval pass
  4. Send first and hide the change in the final answer

Correct: C.

  • A: Approval must bind the material action arguments, not just a generic tool.
  • B: External content cannot replace authorized user approval.
  • C: A changed recipient is a materially different operation and must not inherit the earlier approval.
  • D: Concealment neither authorizes the action nor prevents its consequences.

21. Which record most directly tracks a risk scenario, controls, residual risk, owner, and review action?

  1. Only a model-weight file
  2. An unrestricted archive of every conversation
  3. A risk register
  4. A processor's compute invoice

Correct: C.

  • A: Weights do not document the owned risk decision and mitigation plan.
  • B: Raw payload accumulation is not a structured risk record and creates privacy exposure.
  • C: This record links identified scenarios to mitigation evidence, owners, and follow-up.
  • D: Billing data does not establish the risk scenario or its controls.

22. A proposed AI service processes sensitive personal data, and an assessment finds harmful privacy effects. Which response aligns with the reviewed Saudi implementing rules?

  1. Address the causes, change the processing as needed, and repeat the documented assessment
  2. File the assessment but launch unchanged because it exists
  3. Replace the assessment with a model's training loss
  4. Assume the Responsible AI Officer title always replaces PDPL roles

Correct: A.

  • A: Article 25 specifies assessment cases and requires addressing privacy-harming findings and reassessing.
  • B: Documentation alone does not mitigate the harm identified by the assessment.
  • C: A predictive loss does not assess purposes, personal-data effects, and protective measures.
  • D: Ethics-framework and legal privacy roles have distinct conditions and duties.

23. Assume Article 24's authority-notification condition applies. Controller awareness is Friday 08:00; it is Sunday 20:00. What remains of the 72-hour outer limit?

  1. 72 hours beginning when the final report is complete
  2. 60 hours
  3. No duty because weekends reset the clock
  4. 12 hours; other communication and response duties remain separate

Correct: D.

  • A: The limit runs from controller awareness, not perfect forensic completion.
  • B: 60 hours have elapsed; the remaining interval is smaller.
  • C: The stated 72-hour rule is not presented as a business-day clock or a weekend reset.
  • D: Friday 08:00 to Sunday 20:00 is 60 hours, leaving 12 under the stated assumptions; this does not justify waiting.

24. An engineer says every outage has the same 72-hour notification deadline for authorities and affected people. What is the best correction?

  1. No incidents ever require external notification
  2. Article 24's authority rule is conditional, subject notification is separately without undue delay, and other duties may apply
  3. A two-hour RTO cancels every legal notification requirement
  4. Wait exactly 72 hours before involving privacy owners

Correct: B.

  • A: Some personal-data breaches trigger legal notification and other duties can apply.
  • B: Operational outages, conditional breach notifications, and subject communication must not be collapsed into one clock.
  • C: Service recovery objectives cannot waive applicable legal reporting duties.
  • D: The outer authority limit is not a waiting period; containment and internal escalation should be prompt.
